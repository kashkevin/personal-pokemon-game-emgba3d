# PROJECT UPDATE — GENERATION 6 + GEN 6 MECHANICS

Update the existing Pokémon Emerald Multiplayer project specification with the following decisions.

These rules OVERRIDE the previous Gen 1–5 roster and Gen 5 mechanics requirements.

Do not restart the project or discard the existing architecture/design. Incorporate these changes into the existing plan.

---

# POKÉMON ROSTER EXPANSION

Change the target Pokémon roster from:

Generations 1–5

to:

GENERATIONS 1–6.

The target standard species roster should therefore include Pokémon introduced from:

Gen 1 — Kanto
Gen 2 — Johto
Gen 3 — Hoenn
Gen 4 — Sinnoh
Gen 5 — Unova
Gen 6 — Kalos

This means the normal species target extends through the Generation 6 Pokédex.

Pokémon from Gen 6 should participate in the same systems as earlier Pokémon, including:

- Wild encounters
- Rarity weighting
- Shinies
- Trading
- PvP
- Followers
- Abilities
- Moves
- Evolution
- Mega Evolution where applicable

Legendary and Mythical Pokémon remain excluded from normal randomized wild encounters and instead belong primarily to special/world-event content.

---

# GENERATION 6 STARTERS

Expand the starter selection.

Players may now choose ANY traditional starter from Generations 1–6.

Add:

Chespin
Fennekin
Froakie

Therefore the starter selection now contains 18 choices.

Duplicate starters remain allowed.

Four players may select the same starter if they want.

---

# MECHANICS BASELINE CHANGE

The game's mechanical baseline is now:

GENERATION 6.

This replaces the previous Generation 5 mechanics requirement.

Where practical, Pokémon battles and Pokémon data should follow main-series Generation 6 behavior.

This includes appropriate Gen 6:

- Pokémon stats
- Typings
- Abilities
- Moves
- Move behavior
- Damage mechanics
- Battle mechanics
- Held items
- Evolution methods
- Type interactions
- Status behavior
- Other relevant battle rules

Do not mix generations accidentally.

When there is a mechanical difference between Emerald, Gen 5 and Gen 6, our intended baseline is now GEN 6 unless another project-specific rule explicitly overrides it.

---

# FAIRY TYPE

Fairy type is now INCLUDED.

Implement the Generation 6 Fairy-type system.

Pokémon whose typings changed with the introduction of Fairy should use their appropriate Gen 6 typing.

Examples include Pokémon such as:

Clefairy
Clefable
Jigglypuff
Wigglytuff
Gardevoir
Mawile
Marill
Azumarill

and other Pokémon affected by the Gen 6 Fairy-type introduction.

Implement the appropriate Fairy-type effectiveness chart and Gen 6 battle interactions.

Fairy moves should also be supported.

---

# MEGA EVOLUTION

Mega Evolution remains a major mechanic.

Because Gen 6 is now our mechanical baseline, Mega Evolution should feel like a native part of the battle system rather than an unrelated later mechanic.

Our existing project rules remain:

- One Mega Evolution per TRAINER per battle.
- Each player in a multiplayer battle has their own Mega allowance.
- Mega Evolution requires the appropriate progression unlock/Key Stone system.
- Mega Stones can be purchased.
- Mega Stones should be fairly expensive.
- Players should have to think carefully about which Mega Stone they purchase early/mid-game.
- Additional Mega Stones become easier to collect as the player becomes wealthier.
- Mega Stones may also be obtained through events and special rewards.
- Mega Pokémon can appear in special world events.
- Mega event Pokémon can potentially be shiny.
- Appropriate successful Mega events may reward the corresponding Mega Stone.

---

# IMPORTANT — INCLUDE LATER-INTRODUCED MEGA EVOLUTIONS

The Pokémon species roster is Gen 1–6.

HOWEVER:

The available Mega Evolution roster is NOT restricted to Mega Evolutions that existed during Generation 6.

If an eligible Gen 1–6 Pokémon received an OFFICIAL Mega Evolution in a later Pokémon game, include that Mega Evolution in our project as well.

In other words:

BASE POKÉMON ROSTER:
Gen 1–6.

MEGA FORM ROSTER:
All officially released Mega Evolutions applicable to eligible Pokémon in our roster, including Mega Evolutions introduced AFTER the original Gen 6 games.

Do not limit the Mega list to Pokémon X/Y or Omega Ruby/Alpha Sapphire.

Research the current official Mega Evolution roster when implementing this system.

This should include officially released later Mega forms from newer games/content where their base Pokémon exists within our supported roster.

If a later official Mega Evolution belongs to a Pokémon introduced AFTER Gen 6, do NOT automatically add that base Pokémon to the normal roster solely because it has a Mega.

Bring that case to me before expanding the normal species roster beyond Gen 6.

---

# LATER MEGA MECHANICS

Our battle mechanics remain fundamentally Gen 6.

For Mega Evolutions introduced after Gen 6, adapt them to our Gen 6-style battle engine.

Use their official:

- Appearance/form
- Typing
- Base stats
- Ability
- Appropriate Mega Stone/form requirement

where practical.

If a later Mega depends on a mechanic that does not translate cleanly into Gen 6 battle rules, document the conflict and propose the closest faithful implementation before changing its behavior.

Do not silently introduce an entire later-generation battle ruleset just because a newer Mega came from that generation.

---

# WORLD EVENT UPDATE

World Events may now pull from the expanded Gen 1–6 roster.

Normal event possibilities remain:

- Rare Pokémon
- Mega Pokémon
- Legendary Pokémon
- Mythical Pokémon
- Shiny Pokémon
- Shiny Mega Pokémon
- Shiny Legendary Pokémon
- Shiny Mythical Pokémon
- Extremely rare Shiny Mega Legendary Pokémon where applicable

The underlying Pokémon roster is now Gen 1–6.

Mega events may use any supported official Mega Evolution, including later-introduced Mega forms that meet our roster rules.

---

# RANDOM WILD ENCOUNTER UPDATE

Normal randomized wild encounters now pull from eligible:

GEN 1–6 Pokémon.

Existing rules remain unchanged:

- No normal wild Legendary Pokémon.
- No normal wild Mythical Pokémon.
- Low-level/basic forms dominate early progression.
- Strong evolutionary families can appear early, but generally as their low-level base forms.
- Evolution stages progressively unlock.
- Pokémon use rarity weighting.
- Each player receives independent encounter rolls.
- Shinies remain possible.
- Starters can appear in the random wild pool.

Examples of possible early rare encounters now include Pokémon such as:

Gible
Bagon
Larvitar
Axew
Deino
Riolu
Froakie
Fletchling
Honedge
Goomy

provided their rarity and level are appropriately balanced.

---

# FOLLOWER UPDATE

Follower support should eventually cover the entire Gen 1–6 roster.

That includes shiny variants where applicable.

Mega forms should also display correctly in any situation where a Mega-Evolved Pokémon is legitimately represented in the overworld.

Do not prioritize creating hundreds of follower assets before multiplayer networking is proven.

---

# TECHNICAL RESEARCH UPDATE

Because the roster and mechanical baseline have changed, update the technical-foundation research accordingly.

When selecting an Emerald expansion framework, strongly prioritize one capable of supporting:

- At least the complete Gen 1–6 roster
- Gen 6 battle mechanics
- Fairy typing
- Mega Evolution
- Expanded moves
- Expanded abilities
- Expanded items
- Large numbers of species/forms
- Additional Mega forms
- Follower Pokémon
- Multiplayer modifications
- Large future map expansion

Do NOT continue using a framework solely because it was appropriate for our previous Gen 1–5/Gen 5 requirement if another foundation is substantially better for the new specification.

However, do not throw away existing completed work without first evaluating migration cost.
