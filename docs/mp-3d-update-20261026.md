# Multiplayer and 3D repair update (2026-10-26)

The owner's scheduled update of 2026-10-26: multiplayer stability, responsiveness and synchronization, the 3D world's
rendering and performance, and the overworld getting laggier the longer one plays. No casino work and no new mechanics in it (the Game Corner
changes of the same day are in `docs/casino-economy.md` and `docs/ui-overhaul-and-fixes-20261026.md`). KNOWN_ISSUES MS-71 and MS-88 to MS-94; TEST_LEDGER's section of the same date.

Where a result comes from, always: **L** this Linux machine (real game processes; the 3D view through Mesa's software
OpenGL, so frame times are a CPU's, not a GPU's); **W** a GitHub-hosted Windows runner (real Windows game processes, no
GPU, one machine, loopback); **simulated** the proofs' internet (`scripts/multiplayer/proof_network.py`: a delay each
way, jitter, stalls, slow disk syncs inside the host's process). Nothing here was tried on the owner's PCs or between two
real PCs; no GPU result exists.

## 1. Multiplayer battles: what a choice waits for

### How it was measured

- The game writes a breadcrumb for the battle link's blocks sent and delivered, the commands that arrive, the 3D HUD's
  choices and messages, A/B presses (src/platform/crash_report.c); `PKMN_LINK_TRACE=1` puts each on stderr with the
  machine's monotonic clock, which both games on one machine share.
- `PROOF_FSYNC_TRACE` times every disk sync of the proofs' process (the host's world server and both launchers) on the
  same clock, with who asked.
- `tools/pc_port/link_latency.py` turns the games' runtime.log files into: a choice leaving until the host's answer
  shows; the A press until the first thing on screen answers it; each answer split into client, up, master, down,
  render; link message waits.
- The battles: `native_launcher_battle_proof.py`, the Illusion parties (the host Magikarp, Zorua, Snorlax; the friend
  Zigzagoon), the real launcher, world server and two real games; 120 ms each way plus up to 80 ms of jitter on every
  message, 20 ms on every disk sync (a consumer Windows PC's); the proof's own players press A as soon as a menu waits.

### Before (engine `c33a7fb8`)

The battle and the set-up as above; the 3D game (the Classic 2D game draws its battles with the GBA's windows, so the
HUD's breadcrumbs - the choices, the messages - exist only in the 3D game).

### What changed (engine `bee47749`, the 3D game's battle HUD and the text printer)

1. FIGHT shows the move page at once (the last move menu of that Pokemon, else its party data). A move chosen there
   (keys, a click, the wheel), START (Mega and the like) or B become a goal; when the engine's own move menu comes, the
   HUD presses it as a click does. Nothing is chosen for the engine: its menu takes the same presses a player makes; a
   message or another menu instead drops the goal.
2. After any other choice the message plate says "Choice sent. Waiting for the other player(s)..." from the next frame
   (the move page and the commands: "Waiting for the link..."), until the next message or menu.
3. A or B goes on from a message in a battle between players at once, each game for itself (the battle's master goes
   on when every game has finished its message, as before; the 49-frame wait stays for a player who does not press).

### After

Three runs of the same battle (lat-3d-before-2, lat-3d-after-2, lat-3d-preview-1; 41 to 45 choices each, both games):

| | before (engine `c33a7fb8`) | A/B skip and "Choice sent" | and the move page on FIGHT (`bee47749`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A press to the first thing on screen answering it (median, p90) | **2,983 ms**, 3,455 ms | **32 ms**, 35 ms | 59 ms (see below) |
| a choice to the host's answer on screen (median, p90) | 2,946 ms, 3,419 ms | 2,984 ms, 3,388 ms | 2,982 ms, 3,468 ms |
| ... FIGHT to the move menu (median) | 2,924 ms | 2,835 ms | 2,659 ms |
| ... a move to what follows (median) | 3,060 ms | 3,066 ms | 3,234 ms |
| the move menu's arrival to the move leaving (median, p90) | - | 283 ms, 447 ms (16 frames: the proof's own player pressing A at once) | **82 ms**, 84 ms (4 frames: chosen before the menu came) |
| link blocks in the first 571 s of the battle | 1,013 | 1,023 | 1,025 |

With the move page the proof's player picks its move on the page itself, before the engine's menu comes; the HUD hands
that choice to the menu (the breadcrumb CHOICE 254: 22 of 22 move menus, each with the move chosen). The "first feedback"
column then measures from that earlier press to the "Choice sent" note (up to 0.6 s), not the page's own answer, which is
the next frame by construction. For a person the time to decide now runs while the answer is on its way.

### Where the rest of the time goes

Each answer split at the games' link breadcrumbs (median, ms; the 3D game, 120 ms each way plus jitter, 20 ms a disk sync):

| | client (this game until its block leaves) | up (host and network to the master game) | master (until it sends again) | down | render (this game until it shows the answer) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before | 326 | 149 | 400 | 403 | 1,381 |
| after (skip + note) | 294 | 255 | 19 | 415 | 1,749 |
| with the move page | 359 | 268 | 375 | 382 | 1,757 |

An answer is two or more link blocks away and each block costs about 1.4 s: four one-way trips through the host (the
simulated network's 240 ms and jitter, the host's own handling and its disk syncs: in the preview battle 4,613 syncs, 1,511
of them the friend's and host's launcher checkpoints before each battle command, their directory syncs as many again, and
1,509 the host's battle journal, 22.6 ms each with the simulated disk) and the battle's own pauses on the master game
(messages and animations at normal speed, MS-80). The time moves between the legs from run to run (a game that waits for
the other's block waits in its own leg), the total does not. What would shorten it: fewer trips per block (the link
protocol: the master's answer sent with the delivery instead of after it) and the journal's syncs grouped; both change
the battle's durability and were not attempted in this update.

## 2. The overworld getting laggier

The owner: the overworld gets laggier the longer one plays. Long runs in one game process on **L** (the 3D test build,
Mesa's software OpenGL, `PKMN_DIORAMA_PROFILE=1` and `PKMN_PERF_LOG=1`; `tools/qa/gameplay_check.py` driving the game,
the profile lines binned by place):

| Run | What | Memory (MB in use) | GPU memory, textures | Frame time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| run1 | 350 different places one after another (the first spots of every reachable map) | 304 -> 376, levelling off as new places load | textures 93 -> 169 (new places' tilesets) | no rise |
| loop1 | the same 12 towns 15 times | 292-293, flat | 105 textures, about 110 draw calls | flat |
| loop2 | ten routes' grass, trainers and wild Pokemon, 20 places 10 times at 4x speed (200 places, 81 battles, 32 minutes) | 280 -> 284 | 53 -> 54 MB, 101 textures throughout; draw calls and vertices with no trend | swung with the machine's load (4 cores, load up to 21); no trend |

What grows with the session is the set of places seen (memory and textures level off once they are loaded), not the cost
of a frame. Audited for lists that grow on the per-frame path: the engine's overworld (wild spawns, kept shinies, sparkles,
shared spawns, other players' objects, the bridge, the 3D caches) and the launcher, companion and world host - every one
is fixed in size or bounded.

**Found and fixed** (engine `ea8595b8`): the game queued its sound every frame and never looked at the queue, so a game
clock running a little fast, or a sound device that stopped for a moment, left sound queued that never drained: the sound
fell further behind for the rest of the session. Now at most 8 frames are queued, a stalled device's backlog is dropped,
and `PKMN_PERF_LOG` reports the queue. (On L there is no sound device, so the runs above do not exercise it.)

**Also found, not changed:** the character's profile folder grows by about 3 MB an hour of play (each save's copy is kept)
and the launcher lists it every 1.5 s; not on the game's per-frame path.

**Not reproduced on L:** a frame time that grows with play. A real PC's GPU, driver and sound card were not tried; the
procedure in section 4 collects what is needed if it persists.

## 3. The battle crash (MS-71)

The owner (2026-10-24): the game crashed twice in the middle of a rival checkpoint battle (two PCs); no crash.log was
available.

**Diagnostics** (engine `c33a7fb8`, `a038c3ea`; app `29e2fdc`): crash.log ends with the last 256 breadcrumbs (link commands,
blocks sent and delivered, animations, the battle HUD's choices, messages and boxes, memory), names the engine commit and,
on Windows, the module base and link time stamp; abort(), stack overflows and fatal errors write it too; a failed assertion
no longer stops the release game (errors.log); when a game's own battle core stops itself, runtime.log and errors.log say
which check and why; the packaged games' symbol tables are kept 90 days (artifact `EmeraldTogether-release-symbols`); a
Windows job crashes the test build on purpose and checks the report (`crash-check`).

**Looking for it** (L, real 3D games through the real launcher and world server):

| --- | --- | --- |

| pivot-old-A/-B, pivot-fix-A/-B, status-1: U-turn and paralysis 1v1s | old and fixed engines | MS-90 reproduced twice and fixed (a sprite lost per U-turn on the game that is not the master, and the last Pokemon's status shown on the next) |

## 4. The 3D world, map by map

How (**L**, the 3D test build of engine `5ec3d26f`, Mesa's software OpenGL under Xvfb):
`tools/pc_port/map_inspection.py --reachable-only --warps 2` starts the game on every map one can enter (the list comes from
`tools/qa/inventory.py`), at noon, at the map's middle and at up to two of its warps, and keeps the last picture of each
start. Outdoor maps in three views (the default camera; zoomed out and tilted low, as the owner plays; the Diorama camera

**Results.** 969 of the 972 planned pictures were drawn in 3D, and all 45 extra ones. The Battle Tower's elevator drew in
2D: the 3D view's fallback for a room it does not support, as designed. Three were not taken: the test scene stopped in the
Union Room (exit 139, a segmentation fault), the Battle Tower's multi partner room (139) and the Battle Pike's normal room
(137) (MS-98). The first pass's recorder stopped when the test machine's disk filled (12:03 UTC; one picture was cut short and
three had timed out under load); a second pass took every picture the first one lacked (224, with the three above).

**Every picture was looked at**, on contact sheets of 20. Found: seams and walls where the open sea meets itself and the
map's edge (Routes 105-109, Mossdeep); fixed the same day by engine `d654b78a` (causes in
`docs/ui-overhaul-and-fixes-20261026.md`, section 2). Route 111's plateau had been fixed before the sweep (`5ec3d26f`,

the GBA draws it; Victory Road B1F and B2F are dark until Flash, as in the game.

Not covered: a GPU, a Windows PC, night and weather (noon, no weather set), and walking from one map into the next (each
picture is a fresh start; walks across map seams were part of the render-distance and water work).

### On a real Windows PC with a GPU (the owner's check)

What this machine cannot show is how the 3D view looks and runs on a GPU. A short procedure for the owner's PC:

1. Install the update's installer; start the launcher, Graphics: 3D.
2. Settings: note the window size, fullscreen or not, render distance, zoom and tilt (the owner's earlier screenshots
   were zoomed out, tilted low, on a wide window).
3. Walk through: Petalburg Woods (running), Route 112 and Mt. Chimney's cable car station, Route 111's desert edge and
   rock plateau, Lilycove, Sootopolis, a cave (Granite Cave), Dewford's gym, and ride Mr. Briney's boat once.
4. In each, a screenshot (F12, the screenshots folder) zoomed in and zoomed out; anything that looks wrong: where, and
   what (a hole, a wall in the wrong place, floating things, a flat block, flicker).
5. For speed: start the game once with `PKMN_PERF_LOG=1` and `PKMN_DIORAMA_PROFILE=1` set (a shortcut's environment, or
   a command prompt: `set PKMN_PERF_LOG=1` then the launcher), play 30 minutes, and send `runtime.log` from
   `%LOCALAPPDATA%\PokemonPrivate\launcher\launches\<newest>`: it has frames a second, frame times, memory and the sound
   queue every few seconds.
