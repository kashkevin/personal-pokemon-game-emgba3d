# PROJECT: POKÉMON EMERALD PRIVATE MULTIPLAYER

I want to build a heavily modified version of Pokémon Emerald for PRIVATE PERSONAL PLAY with up to 4 friends.

This is not intended for commercial release or public ROM distribution.

Pokémon Emerald should be our technical foundation, but we are not trying to preserve vanilla Emerald mechanics. We are essentially using Emerald's GBA-style game/engine as the foundation for a custom multiplayer Pokémon experience.

This is a long-term engineering project.

DO NOT attempt to implement everything at once.

Work incrementally, test systems before building systems that depend on them, maintain version control/checkpoints, and preserve known-good builds before major changes.

If copyrighted commercial game files/assets are required, do not download or distribute ROMs for me. Tell me what legally obtained/dumped files I need to provide.

---

# CORE GAME

Base:
Pokémon Emerald

Initial region:
Hoenn

Players:
1–4

Pokémon roster:
Generations 1–5

Target species count:
649 Pokémon

Primary mechanics baseline:
Generation 5

Emerald provides the visual/technical foundation while the gameplay mechanics should generally behave according to Gen 5 unless specifically overridden below.

Mega Evolution is intentionally added despite being a later-generation mechanic.

Do NOT automatically add Fairy typing or other post-Gen-5 battle changes unless we explicitly decide to change the rules later.

---

# MULTIPLAYER PHILOSOPHY

Multiplayer is the core of the game, not an endgame feature.

Once players have selected their starter and entered the world, multiplayer functionality should be available.

Players should NOT be forced into one global multiplayer mode such as "Co-op," "FFA," or "2v2."

Instead, every player exists independently inside the shared world and decides how they want to interact.

Four players might simultaneously be:

Player 1 — Solo

Player 2 — Solo

Player 3 + Player 4 — Teamed

Later those relationships can change without restarting the world.

Players can explore together or completely separately.

---

# FREE-FORM TEAMS

Players can invite another player to form a team.

Normal team maximum:
2 players.

Players can leave teams.

Players can change teammates.

Being connected to the same server does NOT automatically make players teammates.

Potential interaction menu:

- Invite to Team
- Leave Team
- Trade
- Challenge
- Double Battle Challenge
- View Trainer Card

# PLAYER INDEPENDENCE

Each player owns their own:

- Pokémon
- Party
- PC storage
- Inventory
- Money
- Badges/progression where technically appropriate
- Rewards
- Statistics

Teaming up does NOT create a shared inventory or shared wallet.

If two teammates complete something together, EACH PLAYER receives their appropriate individual reward.

Do not take one reward and divide it between teammates.

# WILD POKÉMON

Wild encounters should be randomized.

The encounter system may select Pokémon from ANY eligible Gen 1–5 evolutionary family.

Normal wild encounters EXCLUDE:

- Legendary Pokémon
- Mythical Pokémon

Those use the World Event system described later.

---

# ENCOUNTER PROGRESSION

Random does NOT mean completely unrestricted.

Early-game encounters should primarily consist of low-level BASIC/FIRST-STAGE Pokémon.

Examples of valid early encounters:

Lv. 3 Riolu
Lv. 4 Charmander
Lv. 2 Bagon
Lv. 4 Gastly
Lv. 3 Axew
Lv. 3 Dratini
Lv. 4 Shinx

Examples that should NOT normally occur early:

Lv. 4 Dragonite
Lv. 3 Salamence
Lv. 5 Haxorus

Players may get lucky and find a powerful species FAMILY early, but they should need to raise that Pokémon.

Evolution stages and encounter levels expand with progression.

---

# ENCOUNTER RARITY

Pokémon should have rarity weighting.

Possible categories:

Common
Uncommon
Rare
Very Rare

Not every eligible Pokémon should have equal probability.

Finding something such as Beldum, Larvitar, Gible, Bagon, Axew, etc. should feel considerably more exciting than finding a common Pokémon.

Starters CAN appear in the normal random encounter pool.

---

# INDEPENDENT ENCOUNTERS

Each player rolls their own wild encounters.

Two players walking through the exact same patch of grass can encounter completely different Pokémon.

Example:

Player 1:
Lv. 4 Gible

Player 2:
Lv. 3 Totodile

This is intentional.

---

# SHINY POKÉMON

Normal shiny Pokémon remain possible.

Shiny Pokémon should display properly in battle.

Shiny follower Pokémon should also appear shiny.

---

# FOLLOWER POKÉMON

Players should eventually be able to have a Pokémon physically follow them in the overworld.

Other multiplayer clients should see that follower.

Target functionality includes:

- Choose which party Pokémon follows
- Follower visible to other players
- Shiny follower appearance
- Interact with follower
- Friendship/reaction messages
- Potential location-specific reactions
- Potential occasional item discovery

This feature is inspired by later Pokémon games but implemented in our Emerald-style world.

---

# TRADING

Trading should be available essentially from the beginning of the game.

It should NOT require beating the Elite Four or reaching the endgame.

Eventually the ideal interaction is:

Interact with Player
→ Trade
→ Both players select Pokémon
→ Both confirm
→ Trade occurs

The traditional Emerald Link Room should not be required for ordinary multiplayer trading.

---

# PLAYER BATTLES

PvP should also be available essentially from the beginning.

Players should be able to challenge each other directly.

Target battle formats include:

1v1

2v2:
Player + Player
VS
Player + Player

And eventually:

1v1v1v1

Four independently hostile trainers participate in the same battle.

IMPORTANT:
1v1v1v1 is recognized as a major custom battle-engine feature.

Do NOT assume Emerald's existing battle engine can simply do this.

Research/design it separately and implement it only after the fundamental multiplayer and conventional battle formats are stable.

---

# OPTIONAL BATTLE WAGERS

Players can optionally wager IN-GAME CURRENCY on PvP battles.

Example:

Player A wagers ₽5,000.
Player B wagers ₽5,000.

Winner receives the agreed prize/pot according to the finalized wager rules.

Wagers are OPTIONAL.

Both sides must explicitly confirm the wager before the battle begins.

Nobody should accidentally lose currency because another player initiated a wager.

Team battle wagers should respect each player's independent money.

Do not introduce real-money wagering.

This system exclusively uses fictional/in-game currency earned inside the game.

# GYMS AND TWIN GYM LEADERS

Normal Pokémon Gym battles would become too easy if two humans simply attacked one AI Gym Leader.

Therefore every Gym Leader should have a custom Twin/Partner.

If a player challenges alone:

Player
VS
Gym Leader

If two teammates challenge together:

Player + Player
VS
Gym Leader + Twin/Partner

The Twin is NOT simply a duplicate trainer.

Gym Leader pairs should eventually have coordinated Double Battle strategies.

Examples:

Rain + Swift Swim

Sun + Fire offense

Sandstorm combinations

Status + offensive partner

Screens/support + sweeper

etc.

The goal is for cooperative Gym battles to feel like purpose-built multiplayer boss encounters.

Later Gym Leaders/Twins may have Mega Evolution access.

# GENERATION 5 MECHANICS

Gen 5 should generally be our baseline ruleset.

Examples include:

- Gen 5 Pokémon stats
- Gen 5 typing
- Gen 5 move behavior
- Gen 5 abilities
- Gen 5 damage/battle rules
- Physical/Special split
- Gen 5-style Pokémon mechanics

Do NOT automatically introduce every mechanic from later generations.

Later-generation features should be intentional additions.

The major intentional exception currently is:

MEGA EVOLUTION.

---

# MEGA EVOLUTION

Mega Evolution should exist.

General rule:

ONE Mega Evolution per TRAINER per battle.

Therefore in a 2v2:

Player 1 can Mega one Pokémon.

Player 2 can Mega one Pokémon.

Opponent Player 1 can Mega one Pokémon.

Opponent Player 2 can Mega one Pokémon.

Similarly, sufficiently advanced Gym Leader + Twin encounters may allow each AI trainer their own Mega.

---

# MEGA ACCESS

Players should NOT begin the game with immediate Mega Evolution access.

A Mega Bracelet/Key Stone should unlock through progression.

However, Mega Evolution should not necessarily be restricted to the postgame.

The exact unlock point can be balanced later.

---

# MEGA STONES

Mega Stones can be PURCHASED.

They should intentionally be FAIRLY EXPENSIVE.

The goal is NOT:

"I reached the Mega shop, so I bought every Mega Stone."

The goal is:

"I finally have enough money for a Mega Stone. Which Pokémon do I want to invest in?"

Players should need to choose carefully.

As players become wealthier later in the game, they can gradually collect additional stones.

Mega Stones can also be earned through appropriate events/rewards.

---

# WORLD EVENTS

The shared multiplayer world should eventually generate random timed events.

All connected players receive notifications.

Players must physically travel to the event location.

Do NOT automatically teleport everyone to the event.

Possible event categories include:

- Rare Pokémon
- Mega Pokémon
- Legendary Pokémon
- Mythical Pokémon
- Shiny Pokémon
- Shiny Mega Pokémon
- Shiny Legendary/Mythical Pokémon
- Extremely rare Shiny Mega Legendary Pokémon where the species supports Mega Evolution

Example:

WORLD EVENT

A powerful Pokémon has appeared near Mt. Chimney.

GROUDON

15:00 REMAINING

Or:

WORLD EVENT

An extremely rare Pokémon has appeared.

SHINY MEGA RAYQUAZA

15:00 REMAINING

The exact event probabilities should be designed later.

Extremely valuable events should genuinely be extremely rare.

---

# EVENT MEGA STONES

When appropriate, successfully obtaining an event Mega Pokémon should also provide its corresponding Mega Stone.

Example:

Mega Lucario event
→ appropriate successful reward
→ Lucarionite obtained

Therefore players have two major paths for Mega Stones:

1. Save money and purchase them.
2. Obtain appropriate stones through rare events/rewards.

---

# LEGENDARIES AND MYTHICALS

Legendary/Mythical Pokémon should NOT appear in ordinary randomized grass encounters.

They are primarily obtained through special events/content.

Their event levels should scale appropriately with player/world progression.

Do not spawn a completely unreasonable Lv. 70 boss for players immediately after receiving their starters unless it is intentionally designed as an inaccessible/high-risk event.

# REWARDS

Rewards remain PLAYER-SPECIFIC.

If Player A and Player B are teammates and complete an activity:

Player A receives Player A's reward.

Player B receives Player B's reward.

Do NOT automatically split a single reward between them.

This applies broadly to:

- Money
- Items
- Gym rewards
- Event participation rewards
- Quest rewards
- Championship rewards

Unique Pokémon/event capture rules will require separate design because we need to decide whether a particular world spawn can be captured by one player, each participating player, or according to another rule.

Do not make that decision silently.

# TESTING PHILOSOPHY

Every major feature needs a repeatable test.

For multiplayer systems, test with multiple actual clients whenever practical.

Examples:

MOVEMENT TEST:
- Client A moves.
- Client B observes A.
- Client B moves.
- Client A observes B.
- Both change maps.
- Both return.
- Positions remain correct.

DISCONNECT TEST:
- Player disconnects unexpectedly.
- Other client remains functional.
- Player reconnects.
- Correct state restores.

TRADE TEST:
- Both players choose Pokémon.
- Both confirm.
- Trade completes exactly once.
- Both saves reflect the trade.
- Disconnect during confirmation does not duplicate or delete Pokémon.

WAGER TEST:
- Both players agree on amount.
- Funds are validated.
- Battle occurs.
- Correct winner receives correct result.
- Currency cannot be duplicated by disconnecting.

EVENT TEST:
- Event spawns.
- All connected players receive notification.
- Event appears at correct location.
- Timer works.
- Players can participate.
- Rewards are correctly assigned.
- Event despawns/finishes cleanly.

SAVE TEST:
- Play.
- Save.
- Close.
- Relaunch.
- Reconnect.
- Pokémon, inventory, money and progression remain correct.
